
# Indian Smart Metering & Utility Market Research 2026

## Overview

This project presents a secondary market research study on the Indian smart metering and electricity distribution sector, focusing on smart meter deployment, utility-level implementation, AMISP participation, procurement activity, and project scale.

The research covers selected Indian distribution utilities and analyzes their smart metering initiatives under the **Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS)**.

The study was conducted using publicly available government, utility, regulatory, and company sources, with data compiled and organized for comparative analysis.

---

## Research Objectives

- Analyze the current state of smart meter deployment across selected Indian utilities.
- Compare sanctioned, awarded, delivered, installed, and communicating smart meter quantities.
- Identify major **Advanced Metering Infrastructure Service Providers (AMISPs)** and technology partners.
- Study smart metering procurement and contract/package details.
- Analyze project scale, award costs, and rollout status.
- Understand the participation of major private-sector smart metering companies in India's utility market.
- Create a structured dataset for further market and industry analysis.

## Selected Findings

The research shows significant smart metering activity across the utilities studied.

For example, the September FY2027 dashboard data recorded for selected utilities includes substantial numbers of sanctioned, installed, and communicating meters. The research also identifies large AMISP packages, such as the **13,20,419-meter Andhra Pradesh package associated with Adani Energy Solutions**, with a reported contract value of ₹1,800 crore and a 120-month duration.

For UPCL, the research identifies separate Garhwal and Kumaon smart metering packages, with a combined scope of **16,49,684 meters**.

These figures are presented as source-derived research data and should be interpreted according to the reporting period and source notes provided in the dataset.

## Methodology

The research followed a secondary-research approach:

1. Identified major electricity distribution utilities with RDSS smart metering initiatives.
2. Collected smart meter deployment information from government and utility sources.
3. Recorded meter quantities across different deployment stages.
4. Researched AMISP and technology partner participation.
5. Examined procurement, tender, and contract information.
6. Cross-referenced company and regulatory sources where available.
7. Organized the findings into a structured Excel dataset.
8. Documented source links and research notes for traceability.

---

## Data Quality & Limitations

Smart metering data can differ between reporting periods and sources.

Therefore:

- Current dashboard figures are treated as the primary figures where available.
- Historical figures are clearly identified where they do not belong to the same reporting snapshot.
- Vendor participation is presented as project/market context and should not automatically be interpreted as participation in a single identical current dashboard package.
- Where authoritative data was unavailable, fields were left blank rather than estimated.
- Figures displayed in the source material were retained where reconciliation was not possible.
